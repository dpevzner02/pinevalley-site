TITLE: From FOMO to FOGO: stress-test your own portfolio
SUBTITLE: Paul Singer's Elliott warns the mood could flip fast. Here's a calm way to ask what that would mean for you.

Hi friends,

Elliott Investment Management's October letter says investors could swing from FOMO to "FOGO," fear of getting obliterated. Whether or not Elliott is right, it's a good prompt to ask how your own plan holds up when the mood changes.

## 3 ideas

1. **Elliott's case, and its scorecard.** The letter says "virtually every alarm bell warning of a substantial bear market ahead is ringing," compares today to the Roaring '20s, and points to deficits, inflation, private credit, circular AI financing, debt-funded capex and an anti-AI backlash. In fairness, Elliott returned under 2% through September against about 13% for the S&P 500. Caution has a cost, and being early can look a lot like being wrong. [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/elliott-sounds-bear-market-alarm-134204806.html) · [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/finance/investing/hedge-fund-elliott-warns-of-fogo-fear-of-getting-obliterated-in-the-market-a2cc7027)

2. **The AI funding loop is getting official attention.** Treasury's borrowing advisers called circular financing "a feature" and see tech spending near $1 trillion this year and $1.7 trillion in 2027, with about $735 billion of new tech debt. The cost to insure some AI borrowers' debt has risen sharply since August. [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/ais-circular-financing-is-a-feature-not-a-bug-wall-streets-elite-bond-committee-tells-treasury-155811224.html)

3. **Credit may speak before stocks.** Capital Economics still likes AI's long-term promise, but expects AI investment growth to slow in 2027 and fall about 10% in 2028. It also estimates hyperscaler bonds at roughly $500 billion, about 6% of US corporate bonds, so the risk looks contained for now. [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/how-sustainable-is-the-ai-investment-boom-after-thursdays-selloff-5010263)

## 1 prompt to try

> Act as a calm risk analyst. Here is my portfolio by asset class and percentage (no names needed): [paste]. Scenario: a sharp, AI-led equity drawdown of 30%, credit spreads widen, and inflation stays sticky. 1) Which parts of my portfolio would likely be hit hardest, and why? 2) Which would likely hold up? 3) Where am I most concentrated in one theme, even across different funds? 4) What would I need to be true to stay comfortable holding through it? List your assumptions, and don't recommend buying or selling any specific security.

## 1 experiment: the FOGO drill

Run the prompt on your own holdings, then run a mirror version with a FOMO scenario: markets up 30% and you feel left behind. Write down in two sentences how each result makes you feel and whether your plan, time horizon and cash needs already cover both. Share your biggest surprise in #weekly-experiment on our Discord.

## 1 discussion question

Elliott may be early, wrong or right. How do you tell the difference between prudent caution and costly fear, and what would you want in your plan before you needed it?

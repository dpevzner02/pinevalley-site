TITLE: The AI story moves to the other 493
SUBTITLE: Who's actually getting paid from AI, plus a free tool that reads your open tabs.

Hi friends,

This week several sharp people asked the same quiet question from different angles: if AI is this useful, where's the payoff outside Big Tech? I pulled together the best of it, plus a tool, a prompt and a small experiment so you can test the question against your own work and portfolio.

## 3 ideas

1. **The real test is in the S&P 493.** On Oct 24, Apollo's chief economist Torsten Slok wrote that revenue per worker and profit margins are rising for the Magnificent 7 but staying flat for the rest of the S&P 500. He also sees no sign yet of AI lifting productivity. His bottom line is "the AI story will be decided in the S&P 493, not the Mag 7." [Apollo Daily Spark](https://www.apollo.com/wealth/insights-news/insights/daily-spark/no-signs-of-ai-payoff-outside-the-tech-sector)

2. **Prices ran ahead of cash flow.** Distillate Capital's Q3 letter says the market value of Russell 1000 stocks rose about $32 trillion over three years, while their forward free cash flow rose only about $400 billion. Most of that gain came from the 20 biggest AI stocks, which now make up over 40% of the index and trade near 100 times forward free cash flow. [Distillate Q3 2026 letter](https://distillatecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-letter.pdf)

3. **The headline index hides a narrower market.** The Osterweis Fund's Oct 23 letter notes that the S&P 500 gained 2.3% in Q3, but the equal-weight version fell 1.9% and the Russell 2000 fell 7.2%. [Osterweis shareholder letter](https://www.osterweis.com/files/OSTFX_Shareholder_Letter.pdf)

## 1 tool: ChatGPT in Chrome

ChatGPT's Chrome extension can now use your open tabs as context. It can summarize pages, compare across tabs, and fill forms. According to [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/chatgpt-extension-google-chrome-integration-3724838/), it's available on every plan, including the free one.

*How an investor might use it:* Open a company's earnings release, its call transcript and a competitor's release in three tabs. Then ask for a side-by-side table of guidance changes, and tell it to flag anything it couldn't find. Check every number against the source.

## 1 prompt to try

> You're a skeptical analyst. Below is [company]'s latest earnings release or call transcript. 1) List every claim about AI: revenue, cost savings, productivity or headcount. 2) For each claim, say whether it's backed by a specific number, and quote it. 3) Sort the claims into AI spending (a cost) and AI payoff (revenue or margin). 4) Give me three questions I'd ask management to test whether the payoff is real. Don't give a buy or sell view.

## 1 experiment: the S&P 493 audit

Pick one non-tech company you follow and run the prompt above on its latest release or transcript. Then score it: does AI show up mostly as spending, as savings, or as revenue? Post your score and one surprising quote in #weekly-experiment on our Discord: https://discord.gg/BSwXHrjJS

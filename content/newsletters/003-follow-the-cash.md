TITLE: Follow the cash, not the capex
SUBTITLE: Why Wall Street stopped cheering AI spending this week, plus a model that can read a whole annual report.

Hi friends,

This was the week investors stopped applauding AI spending and started asking who can actually pay for it. Big Tech's earnings told two very different stories, and the difference came down to one line on the cash flow statement. Here's what stood out, plus a tool, a prompt and a small experiment to try the same lens on companies you follow.

## 3 ideas

1. **Cash flow is the new scoreboard.** Reuters put it plainly on Oct 29: "Cash flow is king again." Microsoft generated $18.6 billion of free cash flow in its quarter while spending $45.2 billion on infrastructure, and was rewarded. Alphabet burned $9.07 billion of cash even as Google Cloud revenue more than doubled, and was punished. [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/cash-is-king-as-investors-draw-new-battle-lines-in-ai-spending-boom-4981382)

2. **The spending is enormous, and growing.** TechCrunch tallied more than $740 billion of planned capital spending this year from Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft. Amazon's trailing free cash flow swung to negative $42.4 billion from positive $14.8 billion a year earlier, and the Nasdaq fell 2.9% on Friday. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/cracks-are-emerging-in-the-ai-spending-boom/)

3. **Beware earnings that look best at the peak.** Opal Capital's Q3 letter warns that AI-driven shortages can make some profits look permanent. As new capacity arrives, pricing power can fade, and "a stock that looks inexpensive on peak earnings can look much less attractive on more normal profits." [Opal Capital Q3 2026 letter](https://opalinvest.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-Quarterly-Investment-Letter.pdf)

## 1 tool: GPT-6.1 Terra

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra on Oct 26, a fast model built for visual reasoning, computer use and clear writing. Its API window holds about 1 million tokens, enough for a full annual report plus a few transcripts, and OpenAI says its factual error rate fell sharply from the previous version. It's available to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT, and in the API at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-terra)

*How an investor might use it:* Upload an earnings slide deck and ask it to rebuild the charts as a table, then flag any number that doesn't match the press release. Always check the figures against the source.

## 1 prompt to try

> You're a careful cash flow analyst. Below are the last four quarterly cash flow statements for [company]. 1) Make a table of operating cash flow, capital spending and free cash flow for each quarter. 2) Calculate capital spending as a share of operating cash flow. 3) Note any new debt or share issuance used to fund spending. 4) In plain language, tell me whether the business is funding its investment from its own cash or from outside capital, and what would change that picture. Quote the line items you used. Don't give a buy or sell view.

## 1 experiment: the cash flow check

Pick one company you follow, AI-related or not. Pull its last four quarters of operating cash flow and capital spending from its filings or investor relations site, run the prompt above, and compare the result with what management emphasized on its last call. Post the company's ratio and one thing that surprised you in #weekly-experiment on our Discord: https://discord.gg/BSwXHrjJS

## 1 discussion question

When a company spends heavily on a new technology, what evidence makes you patient, and what makes you nervous? Does it change whether the spending is funded by cash or by debt?

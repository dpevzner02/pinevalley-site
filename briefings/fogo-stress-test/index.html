From FOMO To FOGO: Stress-Test Your Own Portfolio

Paul Singer's Elliott warns the mood could flip fast. Here's a calm way to ask what that would mean for you.

Elliott Investment Management's October letter says investors could swing from FOMO to "FOGO," fear of getting obliterated. Whether or not Elliott is right, it's a good prompt to ask how your own plan holds up when the mood changes.

3 Ideas

1. Elliott's case, and its scorecard.

The letter says "virtually every alarm bell warning of a substantial bear market ahead is ringing," compares today to the Roaring '20s, and points to deficits, inflation, private credit, circular AI financing, debt-funded capex and an anti-AI backlash. In fairness, Elliott returned under 2% through September against about 13% for the S&P 500. Caution has a cost, and being early can look a lot like being wrong. Yahoo Finance · WSJ

2. The AI funding loop is getting official attention.

Treasury's borrowing advisers called circular financing "a feature" and see tech spending near $1 trillion this year and $1.7 trillion in 2027, with about $735 billion of new tech debt. The cost to insure some AI borrowers' debt has risen sharply since August. Yahoo Finance

3. Credit may speak before stocks.

Capital Economics still likes AI's long-term promise, but expects AI investment growth to slow in 2027 and fall about 10% in 2028. It also estimates hyperscaler bonds at roughly $500 billion, about 6% of US corporate bonds, so the risk looks contained for now. Investing.com

1 Tool: Quan 3.5 by StockUp

Released Nov 1, it answers multi-part questions one section at a time, starts from the latest SEC filings, says when data is missing, and can export a 2 to 4 page PDF report (150 credits, $1.50). Free plan: three answers a month; Plus is $12 a month. StockUp

How an investor might use it: Pull debt, interest cost and cash flow from filings for companies in your portfolio. It can be wrong, so check every figure against the filing.

1 Prompt To Try

Act as a calm risk analyst. Here is my portfolio by asset class and percentage (no names needed): [paste]. Scenario: a sharp, AI-led equity drawdown of 30%, credit spreads widen, and inflation stays sticky. 1) Which parts of my portfolio would likely be hit hardest, and why? 2) Which would likely hold up? 3) Where am I most concentrated in one theme, even across different funds? 4) What would I need to be true to stay comfortable holding through it? List your assumptions, and don't recommend buying or selling any specific security.

The Plus-Or-Minus 50% Test

Tech's worst week since 2020 met a record IPO. Howard Marks offers a calm way to ask how predictable your holdings really are.

This week the Nasdaq had its worst week since March 2020, while Anthropic completed the largest IPO ever. In the middle of all that noise, Howard Marks published a memo that asks a quieter question: how predictable is the thing you own, and are you being paid for the uncertainty?

3 Ideas

1. A record IPO and a rough week, at the same time.

Anthropic raised a record $93.5 billion, opened at $111.50 on Thursday and closed Friday at $88.91, close to its $83 offer price. The Nasdaq fell 10% for the week, and the S&P 500 and Dow closed Friday in a correction. Prices can swing a long way in two days even when businesses barely change. CNBC · NPR

2. Every extra "must go right" lowers the odds.

In "A Layman Thinks About AI," Marks notes that being 60% right on each of four calls leaves only a 13% chance of getting all four right, and AI needs even more calls: technology, demand, competition, financing and timing. He calls AI an unusually powerful "fault line" running through portfolios. He also asked Claude to score predictability from 1 to 10, and it gave frontier labs a 2, calling them "close to binary." Oaktree

3. Same numbers, two readings.

Anthropic said it earned $1.72 in revenue per dollar of computing power in the third quarter, up from 68 cents a year earlier, and backers see a high-quality business. Bloomberg Economics' Anna Wong read its financials as a sign of weakening pricing power for frontier AI. Data doesn't interpret itself, so it helps to write down which reading you hold, and why. CNBC · Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance

1 Tool: Gemini Notebook Sites

Since Nov 16, Google has been rolling out a feature that turns the sources in a Gemini Notebook into an interactive website. Type "make me a site" plus a description, and it builds a dashboard, timeline or tracker you can open or share. It's available to Workspace customers and personal Google accounts (18+), with no separate price listed; rollout can take up to 15 days. Google Workspace Updates

How an investor might use it: Load a memo, a filing and your own notes, then ask for a one-page "thesis tracker" showing what must go right for each holding. Keep account numbers out, and check every figure.

1 Prompt To Try

Act as a patient, skeptical investing coach. Here is one holding (a company or a fund) and my reason for owning it: [paste]. 1) List everything that has to go right for it to earn an acceptable return over five years. 2) Score how predictable each item is from 1 (unknowable) to 10 (fully predictable), and explain why. 3) Apply Howard Marks's test: if I guessed its earnings five years out, how likely is the real figure to land within plus or minus 50% of my guess? 4) If my central assumption is wrong, what could I lose, and does this holding add to risks I already carry? List your assumptions, and don't recommend buying or selling any security.

This Week's Experiment: The Predictability Map

List your five largest holdings (or your largest fund's top five). Run the prompt on each, then put two numbers side by side: its predictability score and its share of your portfolio.
